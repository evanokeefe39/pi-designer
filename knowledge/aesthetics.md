# Design aesthetics reference

> Visual styles extracted from design/UI-UX social posts (~28 posts analysed
> as of 2026-06-24). Each entry: what it is, when to reach for it. Use this
> doc when the user describes a mood or audience and needs a visual direction,
> or when they name a style and need its characteristics.

---

## Swiss Editorial

Clean, grid-based layouts with minimalist typography and generous white space.
Rooted in the International Typographic Style movement.

**When to use:** reports, documentation, high-end editorial, formal brand
identity, any project where clarity and hierarchy come first.

**Key traits:** strict grid, sans-serif type (Helvetica/Univers/Frutiger),
asymmetrical layouts, minimal color, photography over illustration.

---

## Brutalist Billboard

Bold, oversized typography, raw unadorned elements, high contrast, often
monospaced or heavy type. Inspired by Brutalist architecture and web brutalism.

**When to use:** landing pages, portfolio sites, music/arts brands,
developer-tool marketing, anything that needs to feel unapologetic and direct.

**Key traits:** huge type (sometimes the whole page), stark black/white,
no decorative chrome, exposed grids, intentional ugliness as a statement.

---

## Cybercore

Early-internet, hacker-culture retro-futurism. Neon on dark, CRT scanlines,
glitch effects, terminal fonts. Nostalgic for the 90s/00s web and cyberpunk.

**When to use:** tech products, gaming, developer tools, AI brands, any
project that wants a digital-native, underground feel.

**Key traits:** dark backgrounds, cyan/magenta/lime accents, monospaced type,
grid layouts, pixel or CRT effects, animated glitch.

---

## Bento Grid

Layout style inspired by Japanese bento boxes — rectangular cards of varying
sizes organised in a balanced, compartmentalised grid. Each card holds one
content type.

**When to use:** dashboards, app UI, feature showcases, brand kits, any
interface where scannable compartmentalised content is the goal.

**Key traits:** clean card grid (often 3×3 or 4×2), mixed-size rectangles,
rounded corners, subtle shadow/separation, each card is self-contained.

---

## Floating Node

Interactive or floating elements (cards, buttons, images) that appear to hover
in space with depth, shadows, and overlapping. Creates a sense of dimension.

**When to use:** creative portfolios, product showcases, agency sites,
interactive storytelling.

**Key traits:** offset positioning, large shadows or glows, elements that seem
to float independently, parallax or mouse-follow motion.

---

## Architectural Split

Divides the screen into distinct horizontal or vertical sections, often
separating text from visual content. Clean diagonal or straight splits.

**When to use:** hero sections, landing pages, magazine-style layouts,
contrast-driven storytelling.

**Key traits:** hard line dividing the canvas, text on one side / image on the
other, asymmetry that forces focal hierarchy.

---

## Googie Kitsch

Mid-century modern futurism — boomerang shapes, starbursts, atomic-age motifs,
bold geometric patterns, space-age optimism. Named after Googie architecture.

**When to use:** retro-themed projects, hospitality, entertainment, branding
that wants post-war optimism and warmth.

**Key traits:** angled roofline shapes, pastel + bright accent combos,
starburst/atom motifs, rounded sans-serif, chrome or gloss finishes.

---

## Nu-Brutalism

Raw, unrefined visual elements that deliberately reject polish. Rough
borders, unfriendly colors, straight-to-the-point layout. A more extreme
evolution of web brutalism.

**When to use:** experimental portfolios, art/design studios, indie products,
any project that wants to feel anti-corporate and provocative.

**Key traits:** raw (not refined) borders, jarring color combinations,
no subtlety, exposed grids, intentionally uncomfortable.

---

## Mixed Media

Combines photography, textures, physical objects, and digital elements in a
layered collage aesthetic. Often simulates print — ink bleed, paper texture,
masking tape.

**When to use:** posters, print, social content, album art, editorial spreads
that need tactile richness.

**Key traits:** layered elements, texture overlays, simulated print effects
(ink bleed, dot gain), physical object scans (tape, paper, stamps).

---

## Pixel-inspired Composition

Formal or informal compositions built around geometric shapes that mimic
the look of digital pixels or low-resolution displays.

**When to use:** game UI, retro-tech branding, pixel-art adjacent projects,
developer merchandise.

**Key traits:** blocky shapes, visible pixel grid, limited palette,
chunky geometric forms.

---

## Geometric System

A structured set of design elements based on geometric forms (circles,
triangles, lines, repeating grids) used to maintain visual consistency across
a composition or brand.

**When to use:** branding systems, abstract backgrounds, pattern systems,
architectural or industrial brands.

**Key traits:** repeating geometric primitives, strict angular or circular
logic, mathematical precision, pattern-driven.

---

## Generative / Procedural

Visuals created by code, algorithms, or autonomous systems rather than manual
authoring. Patterns, particles, evolving forms.

**When to use:** dynamic branding, motion backgrounds, interactive
experiences, any project where the visual should feel alive or infinite.

**Key traits:** rule-driven output, emergent complexity, non-repeating
patterns, real-time generation potential.
